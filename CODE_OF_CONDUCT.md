# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue with the work, not with the person doing it.

## What that means here

This project is about measurement, so most of its conversations are disagreements about whether
a number means what somebody says it means. Those are welcome and they are the point. The
standard is that you criticise the claim, the method or the code, and you do it in a way the
person on the other end can act on.

**Expected:**

- Say what is wrong and what would change your mind.
- Assume the other person is trying to get it right.
- Accept a correction without needing it softened, and give one the same way.
- Respect that people are in different time zones, have day jobs, and are not obliged to answer
  at your pace.

**Not accepted:**

- Personal attacks, insults, or bringing someone's identity into a technical argument.
- Harassment of any kind, in the issue tracker or anywhere else the project reaches.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of that sort.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Sustained disruption: reopening a settled decision to relitigate it, or pressing a point after
  it has been answered.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, in pull requests, in discussions, and anywhere somebody is
representing the project.

## Reporting

Email **ops@themalwarefiles.com**. Reports are read by the maintainer and are not shared further
without your agreement, except where the law requires it.

There is one maintainer, so there is no committee and no appeals board, and it would be dishonest
to describe a process this project does not have. What you will get is a reply within seven days,
a decision, and the reason for it. Possible outcomes are a private word, a public correction, a
temporary block, or a permanent one.

If the report concerns the maintainer, say so, and raise it publicly if you prefer. A policy that
only works when the complaint is about somebody else is not a policy.
